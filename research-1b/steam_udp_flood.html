Unexpected Flood of UDP Packets
Last night i noticed a huge spike in outbound network traffic, which scared the living hell out of me. My first thought was, "I've got malware" but upon closer inspection, it turned out to be the process ui32.exe with the parent process wallpaper32.exe and the following command line arguments:
-parentprocess 5664 -messagehandler WPEhandlerBrowseWallpapers -weuimain -window browsewallpapers -buildv 20000600000021 -skin moss -language en-us -steamlanguage en-us -monitordetection managed -monitormap [Bunch of IDs, redacted] .

While this was happening, I performed a packet capture and saw a lot of UDP packets with destination port 7884 sent to the addresses 192.168.0.255 and 239.100.0.1 . The packet contents were (also redacted): WallpaperEngine/Ping:00000:DESKTOP-NAME:4:Probably-Interface-ID .
After restarting the ui32.exe process, the issue persisted. However, restarting the entire application resolved it.

There's no clear information on what could have caused this behavior, as well as no clear way to reproduce the issue. About half a year ago, I experienced the same issue, but I had no evidence to post at the time. This time, I captured the packets after suspending and resuming ui32.exe to specifically monitor its traffic.
The PCAP file turned out to be about 55 seconds long, with only 13 seconds showing Wallpaper Engine gone rogue. The file size is roughly 37.5 MB.

This issue seems to be extremely rare, and capturing it requires a system monitoring tool. Even if others have encountered it, they probably are not aware.

It's a broadcast on your local network to discover mobile devices running the Wallpaper Engine companion app. It's completely harmless and should not be a significant strain on your system or your local network, it just looks like a lot since that's the nature of a broadcast.

If I remember correctly, this only happens while a mobile device is registered, so if you don't want these broadcasts, simply delete any known mobile device from the Windows app after transferring any wallpapers (and re-add them just for the purpose of transferring). If no mobile device is known, the app will not look for any on the local network.
